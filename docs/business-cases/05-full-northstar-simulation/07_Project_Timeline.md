# BC-006 Phase 1 — Northstar Project Timeline

**Status:** Draft for implementation

**Parent issue:** BC-006 — Complete Northstar Flagship Simulation (#81)

**Scope:** Canonical chronological timeline for the complete Northstar Connected Care flagship simulation.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the authoritative project chronology for the Northstar Connected Care flagship simulation. It translates the six-chapter narrative blueprint into a realistic sequence of project months, learner sessions, milestones, meetings, communications, artifacts, risks, issues, approvals, transitions, and delayed consequences.

All chapter specifications, stakeholder arcs, meeting catalogs, inbox items, documents, activities, and later implementation mechanics must remain consistent with this timeline.

---

## 2. Timeline Model

Northstar represents a **nine-month** healthcare transformation initiative (Content Bible / package authority) compressed into ten learner sessions totaling approximately ten hours.

The learner may complete sessions flexibly. Progression is based on simulation readiness and completion conditions, not real-world calendar dates.

### 2.1 Narrative duration

- Project Month 0: strategic authorization and transition into project initiation
- Project Months 1–2: initiation and integrated planning
- Project Months 3–4: mobilization and early execution
- Project Months 5–6: disruption, escalation, and recovery
- Project Months 7–8: testing, operational readiness, and go-live decision
- Project Month 9: stabilization, closure, and benefits handoff

### 2.2 Learner-session allocation

| Learner Session | Chapter | Narrative Period | Primary Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Session 1 | Chapter 1 | Month 0–1 | Initiation, authority, stakeholder landscape |
| Session 2 | Chapter 1 | Month 1 | Governance, delivery approach, charter confirmation |
| Session 3 | Chapter 2 | Month 2 | Requirements, scope, risk, governance planning |
| Session 4 | Chapter 2 | Month 2–3 | Integrated baseline and planning approval |
| Session 5 | Chapter 3 | Month 3–4 | Mobilization, vendor onboarding, first delivery |
| Session 6 | Chapter 4 | Month 5 | Variance, escalation, regulatory and vendor pressure |
| Session 7 | Chapter 4 | Month 6 | Recovery planning and confidence restoration |
| Session 8 | Chapter 5 | Month 7–8 | Testing, training, readiness, go-live decision |
| Session 9 | Chapter 6 | Month 9 (early) | Stabilization, acceptance, operational transition |
| Session 10 | Chapter 6 | Month 9 (late) | Closure, lessons learned, benefits accountability |

---

## 3. Canonical Milestones

The following milestones are mandatory and must occur in this order unless a documented branch condition delays or conditionally approves one of them.

1. Strategic authorization confirmed
2. Project charter and governance confirmed
3. Delivery approach selected
4. Integrated planning baseline approved
5. Vendor and internal teams mobilized
6. First major deliverable accepted or conditionally accepted
7. Mid-project health review completed
8. Recovery plan approved or rejected
9. Solution testing threshold achieved
10. Operational readiness decision completed
11. Go-live decision completed
12. Stabilization completed
13. Formal project acceptance completed
14. Transition to operations completed
15. Benefits ownership established
16. Project closure approved

Each milestone requires supporting evidence, accountable stakeholders, narrative prerequisites, and downstream consequences.

## 4. Chapter 1 Timeline — Initiation

**Narrative period:** Month 0 through Month 1

**Status:** Existing validated vertical slice. This section records continuity requirements for later chapters.

### 4.1 Strategic handoff

Northstar Connected Care receives executive authorization to proceed as a strategic healthcare transformation initiative. The learner is assigned as project manager and receives the business case, initial mandate, high-level constraints, and incomplete stakeholder information.

### 4.2 Initial events

- Executive sponsor welcome and mandate
- Business case review
- Stakeholder identification
- Early clinical, technical, regulatory, and operational concerns
- Delivery-approach decision
- Governance clarification
- Initial risk and assumption discovery
- Project charter confirmation

### 4.3 Required artifacts

- Business case
- Initial project charter
- Initial stakeholder register
- Initial assumptions and constraints log
- Initial risk register
- Preliminary governance model

### 4.4 Chapter exit

Chapter 1 ends when:

- the learner understands project purpose and authority;
- initial stakeholders are identified;
- project governance is sufficiently established;
- the delivery approach is selected;
- unresolved initiation risks are explicitly recorded;
- authorization to enter detailed planning is confirmed.

Any weak initiation choices must carry into Chapter 2 through reduced trust, unclear ownership, hidden assumptions, or planning friction.

---

## 5. Chapter 2 Timeline — Planning

**Narrative period:** Month 2 through early Month 3

**Narrative objective:** Convert the approved strategic initiative into an integrated, credible, and conditionally approved delivery baseline.

### 5.1 Planning kickoff

The learner convenes the planning team and establishes the structure for integrated planning.

Key events:

- planning kickoff meeting;
- confirmation of planning roles;
- review of charter assumptions;
- discovery of missing clinical, operational, and technical requirements;
- clarification of regulatory and privacy constraints;
- vendor-market and procurement planning discussion.

### 5.2 Requirements and scope alignment

Clinical, technical, operational, and executive stakeholders propose competing priorities.

Key tensions:

- clinical teams seek workflow safety and usability;
- executives seek visible speed and strategic impact;
- operations seeks manageable support and transition effort;
- technology teams seek architectural stability;
- regulatory stakeholders require privacy, security, and traceability;
- procurement seeks controlled vendor engagement.

The learner must establish scope boundaries, decision rights, and requirements prioritization.

### 5.3 Integrated planning development

The learner develops or coordinates:

- scope baseline;
- milestone schedule;
- resource and responsibility plan;
- risk and issue structure;
- quality strategy;
- communications and stakeholder-engagement plan;
- procurement approach;
- governance cadence;
- change-control process;
- benefits assumptions and measurement approach.

### 5.4 Planning challenge

A late executive request introduces pressure to expand scope before baseline approval. The request is strategically attractive but creates schedule, quality, and regulatory implications.

This event must test whether the learner:

- protects planning discipline;
- uses structured change evaluation;
- frames trade-offs clearly;
- engages affected stakeholders;
- distinguishes approval from informal influence.

### 5.5 Planning approval meeting

The steering committee reviews the integrated plan.

Possible outcomes:

- approved;
- approved with explicit conditions;
- deferred pending corrective action.

A deferred approval may not end the simulation, but it must create remedial work and affect credibility.

### 5.6 Chapter 2 artifacts

- Integrated project management plan
- Approved or conditional scope baseline
- Milestone schedule
- Responsibility assignment structure
- Updated stakeholder register
- Communications plan
- Risk register version 2
- Quality management approach
- Procurement strategy
- Governance and escalation matrix
- Change-control process
- Benefits measurement draft

### 5.7 Chapter 2 exit

Chapter 2 ends when:

- the integrated planning baseline is approved or conditionally approved;
- unresolved assumptions and residual risks are documented;
- procurement and mobilization can begin;
- governance expectations are understood;
- Chapter 3 dependencies are visible.

Weak planning must later appear as execution rework, conflict, underestimated dependencies, or unstable scope.

## 6. Chapter 3 Timeline — Early Execution

**Narrative period:** Month 3 through Month 4

**Narrative objective:** Test the quality of the approved plan as teams and vendors begin delivering real work.

### 6.1 Mobilization

The project transitions from planning into active delivery.

Key events:

- internal-team mobilization;
- vendor onboarding;
- role and responsibility confirmation;
- initial technical and clinical workstreams launched;
- delivery reporting begins;
- first operational dependencies surface.

### 6.2 Early coordination pressure

The learner receives conflicting progress reports. A vendor reports that requirements remain incomplete, while internal stakeholders believe the vendor is delaying.

The learner must investigate evidence rather than accept the loudest narrative.

### 6.3 First deliverable review

The first meaningful solution increment or design package is presented.

Potential findings:

- acceptable with minor conditions;
- incomplete traceability;
- workflow usability concerns;
- privacy or security gap;
- unclear ownership for remediation;
- schedule impact if rework is required.

### 6.4 Stakeholder friction

A clinical leader raises concern that frontline feedback is being ignored. At the same time, the executive sponsor pressures the learner to preserve the published timeline.

This creates tension among stakeholder engagement, quality, speed, and credibility.

### 6.5 Early risk materialization

One planning risk becomes an active issue. Candidate issue themes include:

- integration dependency delay;
- vendor staffing shortfall;
- unclear data-migration ownership;
- training effort underestimated;
- privacy review backlog.

The exact event must remain consistent across later catalogs and implementation.

### 6.6 First delivery checkpoint

The learner presents project health and corrective actions.

Possible outcomes:

- first deliverable accepted;
- conditionally accepted with remediation;
- rejected pending material correction.

### 6.7 Chapter 3 artifacts

- Mobilization checklist
- Vendor onboarding record
- Updated RAID log
- First delivery status report
- Quality review findings
- Corrective-action plan
- Decision and action log
- Updated schedule forecast
- Stakeholder-engagement update

### 6.8 Chapter 3 exit

Chapter 3 ends when:

- teams are mobilized;
- the first major deliverable is accepted or conditionally accepted;
- emerging execution risks are visible;
- corrective actions have owners;
- project health is sufficiently understood for the mid-project review.

Unresolved friction and quality gaps must carry into Chapter 4.

---

## 7. Chapter 4 Timeline — Mid-Project Recovery

**Narrative period:** Month 5 through Month 6

**Narrative objective:** Force the learner to stabilize the project under combined schedule, cost, vendor, stakeholder, and regulatory pressure.

### 7.1 Mid-project health review

The project shows material variance or declining confidence.

Possible indicators:

- schedule slippage;
- forecast cost increase;
- unresolved quality defects;
- vendor underperformance;
- stakeholder trust decline;
- regulatory concern;
- scope expansion pressure;
- reduced team morale.

The learner must present an evidence-based health assessment rather than optimistic reporting.

### 7.2 Executive escalation

The executive sponsor and steering committee request a recovery recommendation. Some stakeholders push for acceleration, while others recommend rebaselining or reducing scope.

### 7.3 Vendor performance event

The vendor misses a significant commitment or provides an incomplete deliverable. Procurement and legal stakeholders become involved.

The learner must evaluate:

- contractual obligations;
- relationship recovery;
- corrective action;
- escalation thresholds;
- replacement risk;
- schedule consequences.

### 7.4 Regulatory or privacy concern

A review identifies a material concern that cannot be ignored without creating healthcare, privacy, or compliance exposure.

The learner must resist pressure to classify the issue as administrative if evidence indicates material risk.

### 7.5 Organizational resistance

Operational leaders raise concern about readiness, workload, workflow disruption, or insufficient involvement. Frontline resistance increases.

### 7.6 Recovery-plan development

The learner develops an integrated recovery plan addressing:

- scope stabilization;
- schedule reforecast;
- cost implications;
- vendor correction;
- regulatory remediation;
- stakeholder re-engagement;
- quality thresholds;
- governance changes;
- communication expectations.

### 7.7 Recovery decision

The steering committee evaluates the proposed recovery path.

Possible outcomes:

- approved;
- approved with constraints;
- rejected and returned for revision;
- escalated to executive intervention.

### 7.8 Chapter 4 artifacts

- Mid-project health assessment
- Variance analysis
- Root-cause analysis
- Vendor corrective-action notice
- Regulatory or privacy assessment
- Change requests
- Recovery plan
- Rebaselined schedule or conditional forecast
- Updated cost estimate
- Stakeholder recovery plan
- Executive decision record

### 7.9 Chapter 4 exit

Chapter 4 ends when:

- the mid-project health review is completed;
- the learner has presented an integrated recovery recommendation;
- the recovery plan is approved, conditionally approved, or formally rejected;
- material risks and constraints are explicit;
- Chapter 5 proceeds with a defined readiness path.

Recovery quality must shape testing pressure, stakeholder confidence, and go-live options in Chapter 5.

---

## 8. Chapter 5 Timeline — Delivery and Readiness

**Narrative period:** Month 7 through Month 8

**Narrative objective:** Determine whether the solution and organization are ready for responsible deployment.

### 8.1 Testing phase

The project enters integrated testing.

Key activities:

- functional testing;
- integration testing;
- privacy and security validation;
- clinical workflow validation;
- operational support testing;
- data-migration rehearsal;
- defect triage;
- readiness reporting.

### 8.2 Defect and quality pressure

Testing reveals a mix of critical, major, and minor defects. Executives remain committed to the target go-live date.

The learner must distinguish:

- defects that block deployment;
- defects that may be accepted with mitigation;
- defects that may be deferred;
- evidence required for risk acceptance.

### 8.3 Training and change-readiness gap

Operational and clinical leaders report incomplete training, uneven adoption confidence, or unclear support procedures.

### 8.4 Operational readiness review

Operations, clinical leadership, technology, compliance, and executive stakeholders assess readiness.

The learner must present a balanced readiness position covering:

- technology;
- workflow;
- people;
- support;
- governance;
- data;
- privacy and security;
- contingency planning.

### 8.5 Go-live decision

Possible recommendations:

- proceed as planned;
- proceed conditionally with controls;
- phased deployment;
- delay until defined blockers are resolved.

The simulation must reward responsible judgment rather than speed alone.

### 8.6 Chapter 5 artifacts

- Integrated test report
- Defect register
- Clinical workflow validation report
- Privacy and security validation record
- Training completion report
- Operational readiness assessment
- Deployment and rollback plan
- Support model
- Go-live recommendation
- Risk-acceptance record
- Benefits-readiness update

### 8.7 Chapter 5 exit

Chapter 5 ends when:

- testing reaches a documented threshold;
- operational readiness is assessed;
- remaining risks are accepted, mitigated, escalated, or used to justify delay;
- a formal go-live decision is completed;
- stabilization responsibilities are assigned.

The Chapter 5 decision must directly shape Chapter 6 stabilization conditions.

## 9. Chapter 6 Timeline — Closure and Benefits Realization

**Narrative period:** Month 9 through Month 10

**Narrative objective:** Complete deployment responsibly, stabilize operations, close the project, and transfer benefits accountability.

### 9.1 Deployment and stabilization

The solution is deployed according to the approved go-live path.

Possible conditions:

- smooth deployment;
- controlled issues requiring remediation;
- elevated support demand;
- workflow disruption;
- deferred functionality;
- stakeholder dissatisfaction;
- stronger-than-expected adoption.

The learner must manage stabilization using the consequences carried from earlier chapters.

### 9.2 Formal acceptance

The sponsor, operations, clinical leadership, and technology stakeholders assess whether acceptance conditions have been met.

### 9.3 Transition to operations

The learner coordinates:

- ownership transfer;
- support handoff;
- documentation transfer;
- open-issue ownership;
- vendor warranty or support obligations;
- operational governance;
- performance-monitoring responsibilities.

### 9.4 Benefits ownership

The project team confirms:

- benefit measures;
- accountable benefit owners;
- review cadence;
- unresolved assumptions;
- operational dependencies;
- executive reporting expectations.

### 9.5 Lessons learned and closure review

The learner leads a structured review covering:

- project outcomes;
- major decisions;
- stakeholder relationships;
- risk and quality performance;
- vendor performance;
- governance effectiveness;
- team health;
- improvement opportunities.

### 9.6 Executive final review

The steering committee evaluates:

- whether the project delivered intended value;
- whether closure is responsible and complete;
- whether benefits ownership is credible;
- how the learner performed as project leader.

### 9.7 Chapter 6 artifacts

- Stabilization report
- Final acceptance record
- Transition-to-operations plan and sign-off
- Open-item ownership register
- Contract and financial closure record
- Lessons-learned register
- Benefits-realization plan
- Final project report
- Closure recommendation
- Executive evaluation
- Learner performance summary

### 9.8 Chapter 6 exit

Chapter 6 ends when:

- stabilization is completed or formally transferred;
- acceptance is documented;
- operations owns remaining work;
- benefits ownership is established;
- financial and contractual closure is addressed;
- lessons learned are recorded;
- project closure is approved, conditionally approved, or refused pending specific work.

## 10. Cross-Chapter Carryover Rules

The following conditions must persist across chapter boundaries.

### 10.1 Stakeholder memory

Stakeholders remember:

- commitments;
- missed commitments;
- communication quality;
- escalation behavior;
- whether concerns were heard;
- whether decisions were evidence-based;
- whether the learner protected project value and safety.

### 10.2 Risk and issue continuity

- Risks remain open until mitigated, accepted, transferred, retired, or materialized.
- Materialized risks become issues and retain their history.
- Issues must have owners and due conditions.
- Deferred items must reappear later.

### 10.3 Document maturity

Core artifacts evolve through versions. Later documents must reference prior versions rather than restarting from blank templates.

### 10.4 Project-health continuity

Scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, trust, team health, vendor confidence, governance confidence, operational readiness, and benefits confidence must carry forward independently.

### 10.5 Decision continuity

Earlier decisions may create:

- improved readiness;
- hidden technical debt;
- stakeholder support or resistance;
- quality exposure;
- schedule recovery options;
- reduced or increased executive confidence;
- stronger or weaker benefits outcomes.

## 12. Timeline Rules for Supporting Catalogs

The meeting, inbox, document, and activity catalogs must use this timeline as their chronological source of truth.

Every catalog item must define:

- chapter;
- narrative month;
- sequence position;
- prerequisite conditions;
- whether the item is informational, actionable, decision-triggering, or milestone-related;
- upstream references;
- downstream consequences;
- persistence expectations.

No catalog may introduce an event that contradicts the chapter order, milestone sequence, or carryover rules in this document.
