# BC-006 Phase 1 — Full Northstar Simulation Blueprint

**Status:** Draft for implementation

**Parent issue:** BC-006 — Complete Northstar Flagship Simulation (#81)

**Scope:** Canonical narrative specification for the complete Northstar Connected Care flagship simulation.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document is the authoritative narrative blueprint for the Northstar Connected Care flagship business case. It extends the validated Chapter One experience into a complete six-chapter project management simulation covering initiation, planning, execution, recovery, delivery readiness, closure, and benefits realization.

The blueprint defines the learner journey, project timeline, workplace evolution, chapter transitions, milestone model, continuity rules, stakeholder progression, simulation pacing, narrative principles, and completion model for BC-006 Phase 1.

All later BC-006 documents must remain consistent with this blueprint.

---

## 2. Canonical Simulation Identity

### 2.1 Business case

**Northstar Connected Care** is a strategic healthcare transformation initiative to design, implement, and operationalize an integrated connected-care platform across clinical, operational, technical, regulatory, vendor, and executive stakeholder groups.

The learner assumes the role of project manager and is accountable for guiding the initiative from charter confirmation through transition to operations and benefits realization.

### 2.2 Simulation role

The learner is expected to:

- establish governance and delivery discipline;
- manage scope, schedule, budget, quality, risk, procurement, and stakeholder engagement;
- lead cross-functional teams;
- interpret incomplete and conflicting information;
- make evidence-based decisions;
- manage uncertainty and delayed consequences;
- maintain project continuity across chapters;
- balance delivery pressure with responsible healthcare implementation;
- complete a defensible project closure and benefits handoff.

### 2.3 Experience levels

Northstar must support the existing ProjectSim experience levels:

- **Explorer** — greater guidance, clearer prompts, more visible evidence, and stronger coaching support.
- **Practitioner** — moderate guidance, realistic ambiguity, and balanced consequence visibility.
- **Leader** — reduced scaffolding, greater ambiguity, higher stakeholder pressure, and more complex trade-offs.

The core narrative remains the same across levels. Differences should affect guidance, evidence visibility, ambiguity, and consequence intensity rather than create separate stories.

---

## 3. Learning Philosophy

Northstar is a workplace simulation, not a sequence of textbook lessons.

Learning must occur through:

- realistic project communications;
- meetings and stakeholder interactions;
- evolving documents;
- operational constraints;
- project decisions;
- delayed consequences;
- coaching and reflection;
- visible project and stakeholder outcomes.

PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition concepts must be embedded in authentic project work. The learner should demonstrate judgment, tailoring, leadership, systems thinking, value delivery, governance, stakeholder engagement, quality, uncertainty management, measurement, and continuous improvement through actions rather than memorization alone.

## 4. Six-Chapter Structure

## Chapter 1 — Initiation

**Status:** Existing validated vertical slice.

**Narrative function:** Establish the project, business need, stakeholder environment, initial constraints, governance expectations, and the learner's authority.

**Primary outcomes:**

- project purpose understood;
- key stakeholders identified;
- initial governance established;
- delivery approach selected;
- early assumptions, risks, and constraints recognized;
- project authorized to proceed into planning.

## Chapter 2 — Planning

**Narrative function:** Convert strategic intent into an integrated and approved delivery plan.

**Primary themes:**

- scope definition;
- requirements alignment;
- schedule development;
- governance refinement;
- communications planning;
- risk identification;
- procurement planning;
- quality planning;
- stakeholder engagement planning;
- planning approval.

**Chapter-ending milestone:** Integrated planning baseline approved with explicit conditions and known residual risks.

## Chapter 3 — Early Execution

**Narrative function:** Test the quality of the planning work as delivery begins and the project encounters its first meaningful execution pressures.

**Primary themes:**

- team mobilization;
- vendor onboarding;
- first deliverables;
- emerging quality concerns;
- early stakeholder friction;
- coordination failures;
- schedule pressure;
- communication gaps;
- initial corrective action.

**Chapter-ending milestone:** First major delivery checkpoint completed, with project health clearly shaped by the learner's earlier planning choices.

## Chapter 4 — Mid-Project Recovery

**Narrative function:** Place the project under significant pressure and require the learner to stabilize, recover, and re-establish confidence.

**Primary themes:**

- scope pressure;
- cost and schedule variance;
- vendor underperformance;
- organizational resistance;
- executive escalation;
- regulatory concerns;
- risk materialization;
- change control;
- conflict management;
- recovery planning.

**Chapter-ending milestone:** Recovery plan accepted, conditionally accepted, or rejected based on the learner's cumulative performance and credibility.

## Chapter 5 — Delivery and Readiness

**Narrative function:** Move from building the solution to proving that the organization, technology, operations, and stakeholders are ready for deployment.

**Primary themes:**

- testing;
- defect management;
- deployment readiness;
- training;
- transition planning;
- operational acceptance;
- executive readiness;
- quality gates;
- risk acceptance;
- benefits preparation.

**Chapter-ending milestone:** Go-live authorization, conditional go-live, or delay decision.

## Chapter 6 — Closure and Benefits Realization

**Narrative function:** Complete the project responsibly, transition ownership, assess outcomes, and establish benefits accountability.

**Primary themes:**

- deployment completion;
- stabilization;
- transition to operations;
- final acceptance;
- lessons learned;
- contract and financial closure;
- stakeholder satisfaction;
- benefits ownership;
- executive review;
- learner performance assessment.

**Chapter-ending milestone:** Project formally closed with one of several cumulative outcome profiles.

## 5. Learner Journey

The learner journey must progress from orientation to accountable leadership.

### Stage 1 — Understand

The learner enters an established organization with an approved strategic need but incomplete alignment. The learner must understand the business case, stakeholder landscape, constraints, and delivery environment.

### Stage 2 — Structure

The learner creates the planning discipline required to convert ambition into an executable project.

### Stage 3 — Lead

The learner coordinates people, vendors, decisions, communication, and delivery under real pressure.

### Stage 4 — Recover

The learner responds to consequences, protects value, rebuilds confidence, and adapts the delivery system.

### Stage 5 — Deliver responsibly

The learner proves readiness, resists unsafe shortcuts, and balances urgency with operational quality.

### Stage 6 — Close and transfer value

The learner completes formal closure, transfers ownership, measures outcomes, and establishes benefits accountability.

The learner must feel that each chapter is a continuation of the same project rather than a separate scenario.

---

## 6. Workplace Evolution

The workplace must evolve visibly across the simulation.

### Chapter 1 workplace state

- optimistic but uncertain;
- incomplete alignment;
- high executive interest;
- ambiguous authority boundaries;
- limited shared understanding.

### Chapter 2 workplace state

- highly collaborative but planning-intensive;
- competing priorities become visible;
- stakeholders seek influence over scope and governance;
- assumptions are challenged;
- dependencies become explicit.

### Chapter 3 workplace state

- work volume increases;
- communication becomes fragmented;
- teams and vendors expose practical constraints;
- early quality and coordination problems appear;
- learner credibility begins to matter.

### Chapter 4 workplace state

- pressure is high;
- executive attention intensifies;
- stakeholders defend their interests;
- trust may rise or fall sharply;
- recovery requires visible leadership and disciplined governance.

### Chapter 5 workplace state

- urgency competes with readiness;
- defects, training, operations, and deployment decisions converge;
- leaders seek certainty that may not exist;
- the learner must frame risk clearly.

### Chapter 6 workplace state

- attention shifts from delivery to accountability;
- operations assumes ownership;
- executives assess value and leadership;
- unresolved problems become closure risks;
- stakeholder memory affects final evaluations.

## 7. Narrative Principles

All BC-006 content must follow these principles.

### 7.1 One continuous project

Every chapter must preserve the history of prior actions, decisions, risks, commitments, relationships, and outcomes.

### 7.2 No artificial reset

Stakeholder trust, project health, unresolved issues, active risks, document maturity, and decision consequences must carry forward.

### 7.3 Decisions have consequences

Consequences may be immediate, delayed, visible, partially visible, or discovered later. Phase 1 documents define the narrative conditions; later phases implement deterministic mechanics.

### 7.4 Informational content is not a decision

Emails, meetings, and messages must clearly distinguish information, action, preparation, escalation, and formal decision requirements.

### 7.5 Realistic ambiguity

The learner should rarely receive perfect information. Evidence must be sufficient for reasoned action, but trade-offs and uncertainty should remain authentic.

### 7.6 Stakeholders are persistent actors

Stakeholders remember commitments, respond to project conditions, and change behavior according to trust, pressure, influence, and prior learner choices.

### 7.7 Documents evolve

Core artifacts must mature throughout the project. The simulation should update and reference existing documents rather than create disconnected replacements.

### 7.8 Healthcare responsibility

Clinical safety, privacy, regulatory obligations, operational continuity, patient impact, and responsible technology use must remain credible constraints throughout the simulation.

### 7.9 PMBOK concepts remain embedded

No chapter should become a lecture. Learning objectives must be achieved through realistic project work.

### 7.10 Multiple defensible paths

The simulation may include stronger and weaker choices, but complex situations should permit more than one defensible response when evidence and trade-offs support it.

## 8. Canonical Project Timeline Model

The full simulation should represent a realistic multi-month project compressed into a ten-session learner experience.

### Learner pacing

- 10 learning days;
- approximately 60 minutes per learning day;
- approximately 10 total learning hours;
- flexible completion pace;
- no calendar-based unlocking;
- chapters unlock through completion and readiness conditions rather than real-world dates.

### Narrative timeline

The authored project timeline should span approximately nine to twelve project months from authorization through closure and early benefits review.

### Chapter allocation

- Chapter 1: project authorization and initiation;
- Chapter 2: integrated planning;
- Chapter 3: mobilization and early execution;
- Chapter 4: mid-project disruption and recovery;
- Chapter 5: testing, deployment, and readiness;
- Chapter 6: stabilization, closure, and benefits handoff.

The detailed chronology belongs in `07_Project_Timeline.md`.

## 9. Milestone Map

The simulation must include the following canonical milestones:

1. Strategic authorization confirmed
2. Project charter and governance confirmed
3. Delivery approach selected
4. Integrated planning baseline approved
5. Vendor and internal teams mobilized
6. First major deliverable accepted or conditionally accepted
7. Mid-project health review completed
8. Recovery plan approved or rejected
9. Solution testing threshold achieved
10. Operational readiness decision completed
11. Go-live decision completed
12. Stabilization completed
13. Formal project acceptance completed
14. Transition to operations completed
15. Benefits ownership established
16. Project closure approved

Each milestone must have narrative prerequisites, accountable stakeholders, supporting evidence, and downstream effects.

## 10. Chapter Transition Rules

Chapter transitions must be earned through completion conditions rather than simple navigation.

A chapter transition should require:

- required activities completed;
- required decisions resolved;
- mandatory evidence reviewed where applicable;
- critical meetings completed;
- required documents created, reviewed, or updated;
- unresolved blockers explicitly accepted, escalated, mitigated, or deferred;
- chapter milestone reached.

A learner may proceed with residual risks or imperfect project health when the scenario supports it, but the simulation must carry those conditions forward.

A chapter must not silently erase unfinished work.

## 11. Project Health Model

The narrative must support cumulative project health across at least these dimensions:

- scope clarity;
- schedule confidence;
- cost confidence;
- quality confidence;
- risk exposure;
- stakeholder trust;
- team health;
- vendor confidence;
- governance confidence;
- operational readiness;
- benefits confidence.

Phase 1 defines how these dimensions evolve narratively. Later BC-006 phases define exact calculation and projection behavior.

Project health should not be reducible to one score. A project may be strong in one area and weak in another.

## 12. Stakeholder Progression Model

Each stakeholder must have:

- a stable role and motivation;
- chapter-specific objectives;
- evolving concerns;
- a trust state;
- an influence level;
- a communication style;
- triggers that change behavior;
- memory of learner commitments and decisions;
- potential alignment, neutrality, resistance, or escalation patterns.

Stakeholder progression should be affected by:

- whether the learner communicates early;
- whether commitments are honored;
- whether evidence supports decisions;
- whether stakeholder concerns are acknowledged;
- whether conflicts are managed constructively;
- whether governance is applied consistently;
- whether the learner protects patient, operational, and organizational value.

The detailed stakeholder arcs belong in `06_Stakeholder_Story_Arcs.md`.

## 13. Communication Model

The simulation uses multiple workplace surfaces:

- inbox;
- meetings;
- stakeholder conversations;
- reports;
- project documents;
- Mission Control;
- decision workflows;
- coaching and reflection.

Every communication item must have one primary classification:

- informational;
- preparation required;
- action required;
- response required;
- decision-triggering;
- escalation;
- follow-up;
- status confirmation.

A communication item may support a decision without being counted as a decision itself.

---

## 14. Document Evolution Model

Core project artifacts must evolve across chapters.

Examples:

- the charter begins in Chapter 1 and is referenced throughout;
- the stakeholder register expands and changes as influence and engagement evolve;
- the risk register matures as risks are identified, triggered, mitigated, accepted, or closed;
- the integrated schedule is baselined in Chapter 2 and updated through execution and recovery;
- status reports reflect current project health;
- change requests preserve their decision history;
- testing and readiness documents emerge in Chapter 5;
- closure and lessons-learned artifacts are completed in Chapter 6.

Documents must preserve version history, ownership, approval status, and relationship to decisions where applicable.

---

## 15. Decision Narrative Model

Phase 1 does not implement the decision engine, but it must define the complete narrative space required by Phase 2.

Every major decision should have:

- business context;
- decision owner;
- available evidence;
- missing or uncertain information;
- affected stakeholders;
- immediate implications;
- potential delayed implications;
- reversibility or irreversibility;
- governance requirements;
- relationship to project health;
- relationship to future chapters.

The final decision graph, deterministic consequences, and evidence requirements will be specified in BC-006 Phase 2.

## 16. Success, Failure, and Outcome Model

Northstar should support multiple cumulative outcomes rather than a single binary result.

### Strong outcome profile

- project delivers meaningful value;
- operational readiness is credible;
- critical risks are controlled;
- stakeholder trust is strong;
- governance remains defensible;
- benefits ownership is established;
- closure is disciplined.

### Recovering outcome profile

- project experiences material problems;
- learner stabilizes the project through effective recovery;
- some objectives are reduced, delayed, or conditionally achieved;
- stakeholders recognize credible leadership despite imperfect results.

### Compromised outcome profile

- project reaches deployment or closure with significant unresolved weaknesses;
- value is reduced;
- trust is inconsistent;
- risk acceptance may be poorly governed;
- benefits are uncertain.

### Failed outcome profile

- project is stopped, severely delayed, rejected, or transferred under executive intervention;
- critical governance, safety, quality, or stakeholder failures remain unresolved;
- learner receives a complete performance debrief rather than an abrupt dead end.

No outcome should erase the learner's experience. Every ending must provide assessment, reflection, and traceable explanation.

---

## 17. Completion Model

The simulation is complete when:

- all mandatory chapters are completed;
- all mandatory chapter milestones are resolved;
- all required decisions are completed;
- critical unresolved risks and issues are explicitly dispositioned;
- the transition to operations is addressed;
- benefits ownership is assigned;
- final stakeholder evaluations are generated;
- the learner receives a final project outcome;
- the learner receives a competency assessment and reflective debrief.

Completion does not require a perfect project outcome. It requires accountable project leadership through the end of the simulation.
