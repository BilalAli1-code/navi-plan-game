# BC-006 Phase 1 — PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition Mapping

**Status:** Draft for implementation  
**Parent issue:** BC-006 — Complete Northstar Flagship Simulation (#81)  
**Scope:** Educational traceability between the complete Northstar Connected Care simulation and the PMBOK® Guide — Eighth Edition.

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Northstar embeds PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition concepts in realistic project work. It provides traceability from principles and performance domains to chapters, meetings, communications, documents, activities, decisions, consequences, reflections, and assessment evidence.

Northstar is not a textbook reproduction. Learners demonstrate project-management judgment through workplace actions, trade-offs, and outcomes.

## 2. Reference Basis

The PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition was published by the Project Management Institute in November 2025. Its structure retains a principles-and-performance-domains foundation while simplifying it into six core principles and seven performance domains. It also expands treatment of artificial intelligence, project management offices, and procurement and restores practical process guidance in a non-prescriptive form.

Primary public reference:

- [PMI — PMBOK Guide](https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok)

This mapping must be reviewed when PMI publishes relevant errata or when ProjectSim adopts a newer approved standards baseline.

## 3. Alignment Philosophy

Northstar alignment follows these rules:

1. Concepts are demonstrated through action rather than memorization alone.
2. Multiple principles and domains may apply to one event or decision.
3. No single artifact proves complete competence.
4. Tailoring is required because Northstar uses a hybrid healthcare-transformation context.
5. Learners are evaluated on reasoning, evidence, timing, communication, governance, and consequences.
6. The simulation must not present PMBOK guidance as a rigid methodology.
7. Process guidance may inform sequencing and controls without becoming a universal prescription.
8. Healthcare safety, privacy, ethics, sustainability, accessibility, and responsible AI are integrated into normal project judgment.
9. PMBOK alignment does not replace applicable law, regulation, organizational policy, or professional accountability.

## 4. Six Core Principles

Northstar uses the following six-principle reference model.

### P1 — Adopt a Holistic View

The learner considers the project as an interconnected system of strategy, value, people, technology, operations, vendors, governance, data, risk, quality, and organizational change.

Northstar evidence includes:

- integrated planning;
- dependency analysis;
- cross-functional impact assessment;
- readiness integration;
- recovery-system diagnosis;
- benefits and operational ownership transfer.

### P2 — Focus on Value

The learner connects outputs and milestones to patient access, operational performance, experience, financial value, equity, quality, and sustainable adoption.

Northstar evidence includes:

- objective framing;
- business-case analysis;
- scope prioritization;
- benefits measurement;
- deployment strategy;
- benefits ownership after closure.

### P3 — Embed Quality

The learner plans, assures, controls, and protects quality throughout the project rather than treating it as a final inspection step.

Northstar evidence includes:

- acceptance criteria;
- quality strategy;
- first-deliverable review;
- compliance protection;
- defect disposition;
- readiness evidence;
- formal acceptance.

### P4 — Lead Accountably

The learner acts transparently, respects authority boundaries, records decisions, surfaces uncertainty, protects users and stakeholders, and owns the consequences of recommendations.

Northstar evidence includes:

- governance design;
- accurate status communication;
- issue escalation;
- change control;
- risk acceptance;
- incident leadership;
- ethical closure reporting.

### P5 — Integrate Sustainability

The learner considers the durability of outcomes, operational capacity, workforce burden, access equity, responsible technology use, vendor dependence, and long-term benefits.

Northstar evidence includes:

- sustainable resource assumptions;
- workflow and adoption planning;
- avoidance of prolonged overtime as a recovery strategy;
- accessibility and equity considerations;
- operational support model;
- benefits and knowledge transfer.

### P6 — Build Empowered Teams

The learner establishes clarity, trust, participation, psychological safety, decision rights, and shared accountability across internal and external teams.

Northstar evidence includes:

- planning ownership;
- team working agreements;
- conflict resolution;
- stakeholder engagement;
- distributed readiness ownership;
- lessons learned and recognition.

## 6. Cross-Cutting Eighth Edition Topics

### 6.1 Tailoring and adaptability

Northstar supports Explorer, Practitioner, and Leader modes while preserving one canonical story. Tailoring changes guidance, ambiguity, evidence visibility, and consequence intensity rather than authoritative facts.

The learner also tailors:

- predictive, adaptive, and hybrid practices;
- governance cadence;
- planning depth;
- stakeholder engagement;
- quality evidence;
- deployment approach;
- escalation and communication.

### 6.2 Systems thinking and complexity

The simulation requires the learner to connect:

- clinical workflows;
- patient access;
- technology integration;
- privacy and compliance;
- vendor delivery;
- resource capacity;
- organizational resistance;
- benefits and operational outcomes.

Second-order effects appear through delayed consequences and cross-chapter state.

### 6.3 Procurement

Procurement is integrated through:

- procurement strategy;
- vendor responsibility boundaries;
- onboarding and staffing;
- performance management;
- corrective action;
- commercial claims;
- support obligations;
- vendor evaluation and closure.

### 6.4 PMO and organizational support

The PMO or program-governance function supports:

- planning standards;
- reporting;
- governance cadence;
- escalation;
- approval evidence;
- lessons learned;
- organizational process asset updates.

The PMO does not replace the learner’s judgment or own all project decisions.

### 6.5 Artificial intelligence and responsible technology

Northstar includes AI-assisted scheduling recommendations and AI-enabled coaching as contextual elements.

Required principles:

- human accountability remains explicit;
- privacy and data-use boundaries are established;
- bias, equity, explainability, quality, and monitoring are considered;
- AI may support coaching and dialogue but cannot determine authoritative outcomes;
- hidden scoring and consequence logic remain deterministic and versioned;
- the learner cannot use AI output as a substitute for evidence or approval.

### 6.6 Process guidance

Northstar uses practical initiating, planning, execution, monitoring, control, transition, and closure work. These are applied as context-sensitive guidance rather than a rigid lifecycle mandate.

## 16. Assessment Evidence Model

PMBOK alignment must be assessed through evidence such as:

- evidence selection and interpretation;
- decision quality;
- explicit assumptions;
- authority and governance use;
- stakeholder impact;
- integration across domains;
- communication accuracy;
- ethical and professional accountability;
- quality and risk protection;
- consequences over time;
- reflection and transfer of learning.

The learner should not receive full credit merely for selecting a preferred option. Strong performance requires defensible reasoning and appropriate execution.

## 17. Experience-Level Mapping

### Explorer

- explains relevant principles and domains in plain language;
- highlights evidence categories;
- offers optional hints before major decisions;
- makes authority and approval boundaries visible;
- preserves the same required decisions and outcomes.

### Practitioner

- expects the learner to connect multiple domains independently;
- presents moderate ambiguity and competing evidence;
- provides limited coaching;
- emphasizes practical integration.

### Leader

- uses incomplete or politically framed evidence;
- increases second-order consequences;
- requires strategic recommendations;
- tests governance, procurement, sustainability, and professional accountability;
- may present multiple defensible options with different trade-offs.

## 18. Reflection Requirements

Each chapter reflection should ask the learner to address:

1. What value was protected or placed at risk?
2. Which project-system relationships mattered most?
3. How was quality embedded or weakened?
4. What accountability or governance decision was important?
5. What sustainability, capacity, equity, or long-term concern was considered?
6. How were teams and stakeholders empowered or constrained?
7. Which performance domains were integrated?
8. What consequence appeared from an earlier decision?
9. What would the learner apply differently in real project work?
