# BC-006 Phase 4 — Performance and Reflection

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the Northstar flagship simulation summarizes learner performance, captures reflection, identifies strengths and development areas, and supports transfer of learning beyond the simulation.

## 2. Performance Principles

Performance reporting shall:

- use authoritative evidence;
- distinguish project outcome from decision quality;
- summarize patterns rather than isolated moments;
- explain strengths and development areas with examples;
- avoid certification guarantees;
- preserve learner dignity and agency;
- support replay and targeted remediation;
- remain understandable across experience levels.

## 3. Performance Evidence Sources

Performance evidence may include:

- authoritative decision submissions;
- decision rationale;
- required evidence reviewed;
- meeting and activity outcomes;
- stakeholder interactions;
- project artifact work;
- knowledge checks;
- practice and remediation results;
- chapter reflections;
- final reflection;
- cumulative consequences and ending profile.

## 4. Performance Dimensions

Performance summaries should cover:

- stakeholder engagement;
- team leadership;
- communication;
- planning and delivery approach;
- scope and requirements;
- schedule and cost stewardship;
- risk and uncertainty;
- quality and compliance;
- governance and escalation;
- procurement and vendor management;
- value and benefits;
- ethics and professional responsibility;
- adaptability and systems thinking.

## 5. Strength Identification

A strength should be reported only when supported by repeated or meaningful evidence. Each strength statement should include:

- the competency demonstrated;
- the supporting interaction or pattern;
- the positive project or stakeholder effect;
- an explanation of why the behavior was effective.

## 6. Development Areas

A development area should include:

- the competency requiring attention;
- evidence of the observed pattern;
- likely project impact;
- a practical improvement strategy;
- recommended practice, replay, or learning support.

Development areas must not be framed as fixed personal traits.

## 7. Chapter Performance Summary

Each chapter summary should include:

1. chapter objective;
2. required work completed;
3. major decisions and consequences;
4. strengths demonstrated;
5. development priorities;
6. project-health movement;
7. stakeholder-state changes;
8. unresolved items carried forward;
9. PMBOK-aligned competencies practiced;
10. learner reflection.

## 8. End-of-Simulation Performance Report

The final report should include:

- Northstar ending profile;
- overall project outcome;
- decision-quality patterns;
- competency summary;
- mastery changes;
- stakeholder leadership summary;
- governance, ethics, risk, and value observations;
- most significant positive and negative consequence chains;
- learner reflections;
- recommended next learning actions.

## 9. Reflection Model

Reflection is captured at decision, chapter, and simulation levels.

### Decision reflection

Used selectively for high-value decisions. Prompts may ask:

- What evidence drove your choice?
- What assumptions did you make?
- Which stakeholder was most affected?
- What trade-off did you accept?
- What alternative did you reject and why?

### Chapter reflection

Required before chapter closure. Prompts should address:

- the most important judgment made;
- what changed in the project;
- what the learner would repeat;
- what the learner would change;
- one lesson to carry into the next chapter.

### Final reflection

The final reflection should address:

- how the learner’s leadership approach evolved;
- which decisions had the greatest delayed effects;
- how project value was protected or weakened;
- how stakeholder trust changed;
- which development area matters most next;
- how learning will transfer to a real project context.

## 10. Reflection Quality

Reflection quality may be evaluated for:

- relevance;
- evidence use;
- recognition of trade-offs;
- awareness of stakeholder impact;
- ethical and governance awareness;
- transfer of learning;
- willingness to consider alternatives.

Length, writing style, or vocabulary complexity must not be used as proxies for reflection quality.

## 11. Learner Control and Privacy

The learner must be able to:

- review submitted reflections;
- understand how reflections contribute to feedback;
- distinguish private reflection from shared simulation content;
- receive accessible alternatives for text-heavy prompts.

Reflection data must follow applicable privacy and retention rules.

## 12. Performance Projection Contract

A learner-facing projection may include:

```text
PerformanceProjection
- simulationRunId
- chapterId?
- projectOutcome
- endingProfile?
- competencySummaries[]
- strengths[]
- developmentAreas[]
- evidenceHighlights[]
- masteryChanges[]
- xpSummary
- achievements[]
- reflectionSummary
- recommendedNextActions[]
```

This projection must be rebuilt from authoritative assessment and simulation evidence.

## 13. Guardrails

Performance reporting must not:

- infer personality or protected characteristics;
- diagnose psychological traits;
- claim certification readiness without a separately validated model;
- hide the evidence behind a rating;
- rewrite historical scores after unrelated content changes;
- confuse a favorable project outcome with consistently strong judgment;
- expose hidden branching or scoring details.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria

This document is satisfied when:

- performance evidence sources are defined;
- chapter and final summaries are specified;
- strengths and development areas require evidence;
- project outcome and learner performance are separated;
- reflection exists at decision, chapter, and final levels;
- reflection quality is evaluated fairly;
- learner privacy and accessibility are protected;
- performance projections are reproducible from authoritative evidence.
