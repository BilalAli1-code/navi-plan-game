# BC-006 Phase 4 — Learning Journey Model

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the authoritative learner journey for the Northstar flagship simulation. It translates the approved narrative, decision system, and simulation engine into a coherent learning experience that supports immersion, skill development, reflection, and replay.

## 2. Learning Experience Goals

The Northstar experience shall:

- place the learner in a credible project leadership role;
- preserve narrative continuity across all six chapters;
- require the learner to make evidence-based decisions;
- connect decisions to visible and delayed consequences;
- reinforce PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition principles and performance domains;
- adapt depth and support to Explorer, Practitioner, and Leader experience levels;
- provide a clear sense of progression without reducing the simulation to a checklist;
- support replay, remediation, and deeper mastery.

## 3. Learner Journey Structure

The learner journey follows this sequence:

1. **Business-case orientation**
2. **Role and experience-level confirmation**
3. **Chapter briefing**
4. **Workplace exploration**
5. **Evidence gathering**
6. **Stakeholder interaction**
7. **Decision preparation**
8. **Decision submission**
9. **Immediate consequence feedback**
10. **Ongoing project-state observation**
11. **Reflection and coaching**
12. **Chapter closeout**
13. **Next-chapter transition**
14. **Simulation outcome review**
15. **Replay or targeted remediation**

## 4. Learning Loop

Each chapter uses a common learning loop:

```text
Brief → Explore → Interpret → Decide → Observe → Reflect → Progress
```

### Brief
The learner receives the chapter context, objectives, urgency, and expected role.

### Explore
The learner examines inbox items, meetings, documents, stakeholder conversations, activities, and project metrics.

### Interpret
The learner distinguishes evidence from noise, identifies constraints, and forms a project-management judgment.

### Decide
The learner selects or constructs a response using the authoritative decision system.

### Observe
The learner sees immediate changes in the workplace and project state, while some consequences remain delayed.

### Reflect
The learner explains reasoning, evaluates trade-offs, and receives coaching.

### Progress
The learner completes chapter requirements and advances when all gates are satisfied.

## 5. Chapter Learning Progression

### Chapter 1 — Initiation and Ambiguity

Learning emphasis:

- clarify objectives and success criteria;
- identify decision authority;
- analyze stakeholder expectations;
- avoid premature commitment;
- distinguish assumptions from validated facts.

### Chapter 2 — Planning and Alignment

Learning emphasis:

- select an appropriate delivery approach;
- establish governance and planning baselines;
- balance scope, schedule, cost, quality, and risk;
- create alignment among sponsor, team, and delivery partners.

### Chapter 3 — Execution and Team Dynamics

Learning emphasis:

- lead cross-functional execution;
- resolve conflict;
- communicate with stakeholders;
- maintain team sustainability;
- manage emerging risks and issues.

### Chapter 4 — Control, Escalation, and Recovery

Learning emphasis:

- interpret performance evidence;
- separate variance from root cause;
- escalate appropriately;
- select corrective or preventive actions;
- preserve trust while recovering delivery.

### Chapter 5 — Transition and Value Protection

Learning emphasis:

- validate readiness;
- manage operational transition;
- protect quality and compliance;
- negotiate acceptance;
- ensure value is not sacrificed for schedule optics.

### Chapter 6 — Closure and Benefits

Learning emphasis:

- confirm formal acceptance;
- capture lessons learned;
- evaluate benefits realization;
- transition ownership responsibly;
- assess leadership effectiveness across the full simulation.

## 6. Experience-Level Tailoring

### Explorer

Explorer mode shall provide:

- explicit chapter objectives;
- guided navigation;
- terminology support;
- evidence prompts;
- reduced ambiguity in nonessential content;
- coaching before and after major decisions;
- optional PMBOK reinforcement links.

### Practitioner

Practitioner mode shall provide:

- moderate guidance;
- realistic ambiguity;
- fewer direct hints;
- stronger emphasis on trade-offs;
- evidence-based feedback after decisions;
- targeted remediation when patterns emerge.

### Leader

Leader mode shall provide:

- minimal scaffolding;
- higher ambiguity and competing stakeholder pressures;
- broader strategic consequences;
- stronger governance, ethics, and value expectations;
- more demanding reflection prompts;
- delayed feedback where appropriate.

## 7. Required and Optional Learning Moments

### Required

Required learning moments include:

- all chapter-gating decisions;
- all mandatory meetings and activities;
- required document review;
- mandatory reflection at chapter close;
- end-of-simulation performance review.

### Optional

Optional learning moments may include:

- supplemental stakeholder conversations;
- optional knowledge checks;
- PMBOK concept refreshers;
- replay of completed decisions;
- deeper artifact review;
- advanced leadership challenges.

Optional content shall enrich learning without blocking progression unless explicitly promoted to required content by the canonical business-case definition.

## 8. Pacing Model

The Northstar journey shall support flexible completion while preserving ordered chapter progression.

Rules:

- learners may complete content faster or slower than the nominal pacing;
- no calendar-based unlocking is required;
- chapters cannot be skipped;
- mandatory activities and decisions must be completed before chapter closure;
- paused sessions must restore the learner to the exact authoritative state;
- chapter duration estimates are guidance, not enforced timers.

## 9. Learner Orientation

Before Chapter 1, the learner shall receive:

- the Northstar business context;
- their project leadership role;
- simulation rules;
- explanation of evidence, decisions, and consequences;
- explanation of visible and hidden project state;
- experience-level support expectations;
- accessibility and replay options.

## 10. Cognitive Load Management

The experience shall:

- reveal information progressively;
- distinguish urgent, required, and informational items;
- avoid duplicate decision prompts across surfaces;
- preserve conversation and document history;
- use consistent terminology and status labels;
- prevent overload caused by unnecessary simultaneous alerts;
- allow learners to revisit source evidence before submitting a decision.

## 11. Continuity Rules

The learner experience must preserve continuity across:

- inbox;
- meetings;
- stakeholder conversations;
- decision log;
- documents;
- activities;
- mission control;
- performance summaries;
- chapter transitions.

A completed decision must be reflected consistently across every applicable surface. Informational items must never be represented as unresolved decisions.

## 12. Reflection Placement

Reflection occurs at three levels:

1. **Decision reflection** — optional or required after selected decisions.
2. **Chapter reflection** — required before chapter completion.
3. **Simulation reflection** — required at final outcome review.

Reflection prompts shall focus on reasoning, assumptions, trade-offs, stakeholder impact, ethics, and future application.

## 13. Replay Model

Replay options may include:

- replay the full simulation;
- restart from Chapter 1 with a different experience level;
- replay a completed chapter in practice mode;
- revisit a decision with consequences displayed for learning purposes;
- target a development area identified by the assessment system.

Replay must never overwrite the historical record of the original completed run.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria

This document is satisfied when:

- all six chapters have explicit learning emphasis;
- the common learning loop is implemented consistently;
- experience-level tailoring is visible but does not fork the canonical story;
- mandatory and optional learning moments are distinguishable;
- chapter order and no-skipping rules are enforced;
- cross-surface learner continuity is preserved;
- reflection and replay are supported;
- the journey remains aligned with the authoritative narrative, decision, and engine specifications.
