# BC-006 Phase 4 — Progression Experience

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how learners perceive progress, achievement, and momentum throughout the Northstar flagship simulation. Progression must motivate continued engagement while remaining subordinate to meaningful project-management learning.

## 2. Progression Principles

Progression shall:

- reflect authentic completion and demonstrated learning;
- avoid rewarding superficial clicking;
- preserve the distinction between simulation progress and competency mastery;
- use consistent status across all surfaces;
- support flexible pacing;
- provide clear next actions;
- remain accessible and understandable;
- avoid manipulative or punitive engagement patterns.

## 3. Progression Dimensions

The learner experience uses four separate progression dimensions:

1. **Simulation completion** — movement through chapters and required work.
2. **Project outcome** — health and final state of the Northstar project.
3. **Learning performance** — quality of decisions, activities, and reflection.
4. **Mastery development** — cumulative competency growth across evidence.

These dimensions must not be collapsed into a single percentage.

## 4. Chapter Progress

Chapter progress is calculated from authoritative requirements only.

A chapter progress projection may include:

- required decisions completed;
- required meetings completed;
- required activities completed;
- required documents reviewed or produced;
- required reflections completed;
- chapter-exit conditions satisfied.

Informational items must not inflate or block progress.

## 5. Completion Statuses

Canonical statuses are:

- `not_started`
- `available`
- `in_progress`
- `blocked`
- `ready_to_complete`
- `completed`

A completed item must appear completed everywhere it is referenced. A decision must never appear both completed and pending across different surfaces.

## 6. XP Model

XP represents meaningful learning participation and accomplishment. XP may be awarded for:

- completing required activities;
- submitting authoritative decisions;
- demonstrating strong reasoning;
- completing reflections;
- improving through remediation;
- finishing chapters;
- completing the simulation;
- earning achievements.

XP must not be awarded repeatedly for the same immutable event. Exactly-once rules apply.

## 7. XP Guardrails

The system must prevent:

- duplicate XP on retries;
- farming through repeated opening or closing of content;
- excessive reward for optional low-value actions;
- XP deductions that obscure learning feedback;
- XP totals being represented as certification readiness.

## 8. Levels

Learner levels may summarize accumulated XP across the product. Levels should:

- use stable thresholds;
- unlock recognition or optional content rather than core simulation access;
- avoid implying professional authority or certification;
- remain separate from Explorer, Practitioner, and Leader simulation modes.

Experience level describes the selected simulation challenge. Learner level describes product-wide progression. These concepts must never be conflated.

## 9. Achievements

Achievements recognize meaningful patterns such as:

- evidence-based decision making;
- stakeholder alignment;
- effective escalation;
- ethical leadership;
- risk anticipation;
- quality protection;
- team sustainability;
- value realization;
- successful recovery;
- thoughtful reflection.

Achievements must have:

- stable identifiers;
- explicit criteria;
- exactly-once award behavior per applicable scope;
- explanatory learner-facing text;
- evidence references;
- accessibility-compatible presentation.

## 10. Chapter Completion Experience

When a chapter is completed, the learner should receive:

- confirmation of completion;
- summary of major decisions;
- project-state changes;
- stakeholder developments;
- strengths demonstrated;
- development areas;
- XP and achievements earned;
- unresolved risks or consequences carried forward;
- preview of the next chapter.

The completion experience must not reveal hidden future events.

## 11. Mission Control Requirements

Mission Control should show:

- current chapter and objective;
- authoritative completion status;
- required work remaining;
- pending decision count;
- current project-health summary;
- relevant stakeholder or risk signals;
- next recommended action.

It must not duplicate equivalent sections that create conflicting counts or unclear priorities.

## 12. Motivation Without Distortion

Motivational design may include:

- visible milestones;
- progress acknowledgements;
- achievement moments;
- chapter previews;
- recovery encouragement;
- evidence of learner improvement.

It must not include:

- false urgency;
- streak pressure that penalizes flexible pacing;
- random rewards disconnected from learning;
- hidden penalties for pauses;
- misleading success claims.

## 13. Pause and Resume

On resume, the learner must see:

- current chapter;
- last completed meaningful action;
- unresolved required items;
- important project changes already observed;
- next recommended action.

Resume projections must be rebuilt from authoritative state and must not depend solely on client memory.

## 14. End-of-Simulation Progression

After final closure, the learner receives:

- ending profile;
- project outcome;
- competency summary;
- mastery changes;
- total XP earned;
- achievements;
- recommended next scenario or replay path;
- preserved historical run record.

## 15. Acceptance Criteria

This document is satisfied when:

- completion, outcome, performance, and mastery are presented separately;
- progress derives only from authoritative requirements;
- statuses converge across all surfaces;
- XP and achievements follow exactly-once rules;
- learner level and experience level remain distinct;
- chapter completion provides meaningful feedback and a next step;
- pause and resume are reliable;
- motivation supports learning without manipulative engagement mechanics.
