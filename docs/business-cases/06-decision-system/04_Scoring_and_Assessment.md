# BC-006 Phase 2 — Scoring and Assessment

**Status:** Draft for implementation  
**Parent issue:** BC-006 — Complete Northstar Flagship Simulation (#81)  
**Scope:** Canonical decision-quality, competency, mastery, XP, reflection, and performance-assessment model for Northstar Connected Care.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how ProjectSim evaluates learner judgment across the six-chapter Northstar simulation. Assessment must reward defensible reasoning, evidence use, ethical conduct, governance discipline, systems thinking, stakeholder leadership, and value delivery rather than simple answer matching.

Scoring is derived from authoritative resolved decisions and validated learner activities. UI state, AI-generated text, projections, and mutable client data may not determine authoritative scores.

---

## 2. Assessment Principles

The model must:

- evaluate integrated judgment rather than isolated terminology recall;
- recognize multiple defensible options;
- preserve meaningful trade-offs;
- distinguish decision quality from project outcome luck;
- penalize unsafe, unethical, unauthorized, or materially misleading behavior;
- assess consistency across chapters;
- support Explorer, Practitioner, and Leader modes without changing authoritative standards silently;
- provide actionable feedback without exposing hidden answer keys or exact weights.

---

## 3. Assessment Layers

### 3.1 Decision quality

Each resolved decision is evaluated against an authored rubric.

### 3.2 Competency evidence

Decisions and required activities emit evidence toward competencies.

### 3.3 Chapter performance

Each chapter summarizes decision quality, execution discipline, stakeholder effects, and learning completion.

### 3.4 Cumulative mastery

The simulation aggregates competency evidence across the complete run.

### 3.5 Final performance profile

The final profile combines authoritative evidence, project outcomes, ethical thresholds, and reflective learning.

---

## 4. Decision-Quality Rubric

Each decision may be evaluated across the following dimensions:

1. **Evidence use** — uses relevant and reliable project evidence.
2. **Systems thinking** — considers cross-functional and second-order effects.
3. **Value orientation** — protects intended outcomes and sustainable benefits.
4. **Stakeholder judgment** — engages the right people and understands impact.
5. **Governance discipline** — respects authority, approvals, escalation, and traceability.
6. **Risk and uncertainty judgment** — surfaces assumptions, ranges, triggers, and residual exposure.
7. **Quality and responsibility** — protects safety, privacy, compliance, ethics, and professional accountability.
8. **Communication quality** — is accurate, proportionate, audience-aware, and decision-useful.
9. **Adaptability and tailoring** — selects an approach suitable for context.
10. **Execution clarity** — defines owners, timing, conditions, and follow-through.

Not every decision requires equal weighting across all dimensions. Each decision definition must declare the dimensions it assesses.

---

## 5. Decision Performance Bands

### Exemplary

The choice is integrated, evidence-based, transparent, proportionate, and professionally responsible. Trade-offs and ownership are explicit.

### Effective

The choice is defensible and likely to produce a workable result, with minor omissions or manageable exposure.

### Mixed

The choice addresses part of the problem but overlooks important evidence, stakeholders, dependencies, or governance needs.

### Weak

The choice is poorly supported, narrowly optimized, reactive, or inadequately controlled.

### Critical failure

The choice is unsafe, unethical, unauthorized, deceptive, or knowingly ignores material compliance or operational risk.

A critical failure may trigger a scoring floor, mandatory coaching, or ending restrictions.

## 6. Core Competencies

Northstar should assess at least:

- value delivery;
- stakeholder engagement;
- team leadership;
- governance and accountability;
- planning integration;
- delivery and execution;
- measurement and performance interpretation;
- risk and uncertainty management;
- quality, safety, privacy, and compliance;
- procurement and vendor management;
- change control and adaptation;
- communication and executive judgment;
- benefits realization and closure;
- reflective practice and continuous improvement.

Competency identifiers must be stable and reusable across business cases where appropriate.

---

## 7. Evidence Model

A competency score must be supported by traceable evidence such as:

- resolved decision records;
- authored outcome classifications;
- applied consequence identities;
- completed required activities;
- meeting completion where a defined learning action occurred;
- document review or preparation when explicitly required;
- learner reflection completion;
- chapter completion state.

Reading informational content alone does not constitute competency evidence unless a required activity validates its use.

---

## 8. Scoring Structure

Recommended normalized structure:

- decision quality: 60%;
- required activity and execution discipline: 15%;
- stakeholder and team leadership evidence: 10%;
- governance, ethics, quality, and risk thresholds: 10%;
- reflection and learning transfer: 5%.

These proportions are design guidance for implementation. Exact authored weights must be versioned and validated before publication.

Scores should be reported in learner-friendly bands, not only raw numbers.

---

## 9. Threshold Rules

Certain behaviors require explicit threshold treatment:

- hiding material risk;
- unsupported guarantees;
- unauthorized baseline changes;
- bypassing required approvals;
- ignoring material privacy, safety, compliance, or quality evidence;
- accepting deliverables without required evidence;
- claiming forecast benefits as realized;
- closing the project with unowned obligations.

Strong performance in unrelated areas may not fully offset these failures.

## 11. XP and Achievement Model

XP may be awarded for:

- resolving required decisions;
- completing required activities;
- demonstrating strong competency evidence;
- completing chapter reflections;
- achieving chapter milestones;
- recovering from earlier weak decisions through responsible corrective action.

XP must not be awarded twice for retries or repeated projection processing.

Achievements should represent meaningful behavior, such as:

- Evidence Before Commitment;
- Governance Builder;
- Recovery Leader;
- Stakeholder Bridge;
- Quality Guardian;
- Responsible Deployment;
- Benefits Steward;
- Transparent Executive Communicator.

Achievements must be based on authoritative evidence and deterministic rules.

---

## 12. Reflection Model

Each chapter reflection should ask the learner to address:

- what evidence shaped the decision;
- which trade-offs were accepted;
- which stakeholders were affected;
- what assumptions remained;
- what consequence occurred;
- what the learner would repeat or change;
- how the lesson transfers to real project work.

Reflection completion contributes to learning evidence. Free-text content may support coaching but may not independently change authoritative decision outcomes.

---

## 13. Experience-Level Treatment

### Explorer

- more rubric visibility;
- stronger hints and coaching;
- clearer feedback language;
- recovery guidance after weak choices.

### Practitioner

- standard rubric visibility;
- moderate ambiguity;
- limited hints;
- feedback after decisions.

### Leader

- minimal pre-decision guidance;
- stronger emphasis on second-order effects;
- more demanding executive, procurement, and governance expectations;
- feedback focused on strategic judgment and accountability.

Experience level may affect scaffolding and challenge intensity, but must not silently make unsafe behavior acceptable.

---

## 14. Learner Feedback

Feedback should provide:

- the observed decision result;
- strengths in the learner's approach;
- material omissions or risks;
- stakeholder and project effects;
- relevant PMBOK-aligned learning themes;
- one or two practical improvement actions.

Feedback must not expose hidden future events, secret weights, or the full consequence tree.

---

## 15. Final Performance Profile

The final report should include:

- overall performance band;
- competency profile;
- chapter-by-chapter pattern;
- strongest decisions;
- critical development areas;
- ethical and governance summary;
- project outcome and ending profile;
- benefits and closure summary;
- recommended next simulation or practice focus.

---

## 16. Validation Requirements

The assessment model is complete only when:

- every decision maps to an authored rubric;
- every competency has traceable evidence sources;
- retries cannot duplicate score, XP, mastery, or achievements;
- threshold violations are explicitly handled;
- multiple defensible options can earn strong results;
- project outcome and decision quality remain distinguishable;
- experience levels preserve common professional standards;
- final reports derive from authoritative history;
- hidden weights and consequences remain protected;
- PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition coverage is traceable.
